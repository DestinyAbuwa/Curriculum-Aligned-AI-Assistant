A 7-year-old female is referred to an endocrinologist for below normal growth. Labs show a normal GH release in response to exercise, but low levels of serum IGF-1. Which of the following is the likely mechanism underlying her lack of growth? - A.

--- CHUNK BREAK ---

Decreased GHRH secretion
- B. Decreased GH receptor expression on bone
- C. Decreased GH receptor sensitivity
- D. Decreased GH synthesis in the anterior pituitary

The low levels of IGF-1 indicate a problem with IGF-1 production in response to GH release. Since GH release is normal in response to exercise, GH synthesis and GHRH secretion must be normal. Therefore, the likely mechanism is diminished GH receptor sensitivity, meaning that less IGF-1 is produced in response to GH in the liver. GH's effects on bone are mostly mediated through IGF-1, so decreased GH receptors on bone cannot be the mechanism. <!-- image -->

## Growth abnormalities due to GH excess or deficiency

- Before adulthood:
- Growth impairment
- Isolated GH deficiency (mutations in GH/GHRH genes)
- Laron Syndrome: GH resistance due to mutation of the GH receptor
- Gigantism
- Excess GH
- After adulthood (closure of epiphyses):
- No impairment of longitudinal growth
- Excess GH causes acromegaly (appositional growth)

## GH is considered a diabetogenic hormone: Mechanisms involved in GH-mediated hyperglycemia

1. Decreased glucose uptake into muscle and adipose tissue
- due to increased serum FFA from lipolysis

## 2. Stimulation of liver gluconeogenesis

- Increased liver acetyl CoA from fatty acid oxidation
- GH-mediated increase in PEPCK expression

## 3. Insulin insensitivity

- GH antagonizes the action of insulin at the post-receptor level in skeletal muscle &amp; adipose tissue
