## Diagnosis of Diabetes (ADA)

A hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) level of 6.5% or higher; or

A fasting plasma glucose (FPG) level of 126 mg/dL (7 mmol/L) or higher; fasting is defined as no caloric intake for at least 8 hours, or

A 2-hour plasma glucose level of 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or higher during a 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), or

A random plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or higher in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia (i.e., polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss) or hyperglycemic crisis

## Pharmacology of Insulin

- Insulin preparations differ in:  onset and duration of action
- Humulins:  recombinant DNA synthesis of human insulin
- Insulin rapidly metabolized, not given orally
- Insulin preps classified as ultra-rapid-, rapid-, short-, intermediate- or long-acting
- duration prolonged by conjugation with protein, crystallization with zinc, or AA substitutions
- Effect on Weight: Increase
- HbA1c: &gt; 2% reduction (most effective)
- Side effects: hypoglycemia, weight gain, injection site discomfort

## The Basal/Bolus Insulin Concept

## Basal Insulin

- Suppresses glucose production between meals and overnight
- Nearly constant levels
- 40 to 50% of daily needs

## Bolus Insulin (Mealtime or Prandial)

- Limits hyperglycemia after meals
- Immediate rise and sharp peak at 1 hour
- 10% to 20% of total daily insulin requirement at each meal

Ideally, for insulin replacement therapy, each component should come from a different insulin with a specific profile…or is delivered with a system that allows for physiological matching. <!-- image -->

## SGLT2 inhibitors

Agents: Canagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin , Ertugliflozin, Bexagliflozin

MOA: SGLT2 (sodium glucose transporter) responsible in reabsorption of glucose in proximal tubule of the kidney. SGLT2 inhibitors reduce glucose reabsorption, thus increasing glycosuria

Effect on weight: 2- 3Kg reduction

Hba1c: 0.5-1% reduction

## Adverse reactions:

## Classification of Hypoglycemia

Level 1 : A glucose alert value of 70 mg/dL or less. Level 2 : A glucose level of 54 mg/dL is sufficiently low to indicate potentially serious, clinically important hypoglycemia. Level 3 : Severe hypoglycemia, as defined by the ADA, denotes severe cognitive impairment requiring external assistance for recovery. Ibrahim M, Baker J, Cahn A, et al. ## Hypoglycemia and Intervention

## Hypoglycemia

- Common complication
- Major limiting factors to optimal glycemic control

## Intervention

- 'rule of 15'
- 15 gram of fast-acting carbohydrates (1/2 cup of milk/juice/soda)
- Check glucose in 15 minutes, if glucose below 70mg/dl, repeat
- If glucose is above 70mg/dl, eat a normal snack or meal

## Key Points: Drugs for T2DM

- Metformin is the typical initial agent, provided there is no contraindication for its use or compelling reasons for an alternative agent
- Multiple classes of agents are available as add-on agents or if metformin is not an option
- Each class of anti-diabetes medications has its own good and bad features
- Agents such as metformin, GLP-1R agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors, and SGLT2 inhibitors are weight neutral or have potential for weight loss
